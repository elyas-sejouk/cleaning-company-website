# 0004 Réalisations Are Curated Evidence, Not a Complete Archive

The `/realisations` page publishes **23 of the 42 available photographs**, and every published frame carries a caption. The photographs were taken as trade documentation — proof to a client that a named surface was cleaned — not as marketing images, so a substantial minority are unusable in a public gallery: some are correctly labelled "après" while framing a courtyard full of ladders and rubble, some are close-ups whose subject is unidentifiable without the filename, and some are near-duplicates. A visitor cannot distinguish "this photograph documents clean glass" from "this is their idea of clean." Publishing everything would subtract trust rather than add it, so we curate deliberately and caption each survivor to direct the eye to the subject.

## Considered Options

- **Publish all 42.** Rejected. The failure is not that weak frames are merely neutral — a gallery in which half the "après" shots look like building sites actively undermines the **Prestation** claims it is meant to support.
- **Publish only genuine Avant/Après pairs.** Rejected. Only four to six true pairs exist across the whole set, and the two largest Réalisations (Alignan-du-Vent, 19 frames) have essentially none. This would discard the strongest evidence to satisfy a format.
- **Pair unrelated "avant" and "après" frames from the same job.** Rejected on integrity grounds. A slider implicitly asserts that both frames show the same spot; pairing a dirty terrace with a different clean terrace is a fabricated before/after, and it is trivially disprovable.

## Consequences

- **The gap between 42 files and 23 published frames is deliberate.** It is not an oversight and must not be "fixed" by restoring the cut frames. The cut list and the reasoning behind each exclusion are recorded alongside the curation.
- Captions are load-bearing, not decorative. A frame whose subject is ambiguous without its caption should be cut, not shipped uncaptioned.
- Filenames are not authoritative. At least one pair (`vitre - Anti calcaire` / `Vitre avant` at Alignan-du-Vent) has its labels inverted relative to the capture timestamps, which would drive a slider backwards. Trust EXIF over filenames.
- The Kärcher Réalisation at Cers survives on a **Mi-Parcours** frame rather than an Avant/Après pair — its four "après" frames were visually indistinguishable from its "avant" frames. Where a transformation is invisible to an untrained eye, the half-finished frame is the stronger evidence, and it is the format the field team should be shooting for.
- Entries deliberately vary in weight — seven frames at Coursan, two at Cers. The journal layout was chosen partly so that a sparse entry reads as a short job rather than as a broken card.
